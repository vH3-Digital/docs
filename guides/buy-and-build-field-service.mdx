Private equity firms often build UK field service groups by buying regional contractors. The commercial case is clear. The operating model is harder.

Each bolt-on brings its own FMS setup, shared inboxes, portal mail, job-type names, and local workarounds. The board needs one view of the group. Operators still work in several different versions of how work arrives and how it gets done.

VH3 AI reads the systems each company already runs, connects jobs and sites across brands, and watches the group for problems. Ops leads and the board work from the same answers. The intelligence lives in each company's account.

## What breaks after each acquisition

A typical UK bolt-on arrives with:

- An FMS that already runs jobs, often a different product or a different configuration of the same product
- One or more shared inboxes handling repairs, POs, quotes, and portal notifications
- Local job-type and destination vocabulary that does not match the platform company's
- Spreadsheet trackers beside the FMS for SLAs, callbacks, and account reviews
- Engineers and coordinators who know the exceptions that never made it into a policy document

These are normal working practices for regional contractors. They become a group problem after acquisition. Weekly board packs turn into a manual merge of exports. "Emergency" means something different in each brand. A shared inbox still depends on the person who knows which folder to use. Revenue and headcount grow, but the group still lacks a consistent operating view.

Start by answering four operational questions:

- Which mailboxes are shared or team inboxes, and which stay individual?
- Which job types and note types should the platform ignore?
- Which portals create work, and which emails are noise?
- What does the board need every week that ops can actually produce?

## The operating problem this guide covers

Sponsors usually fund two different types of work.

**Engineering velocity.** This gives the portfolio company more software delivery capacity, shorter lead times, and suitable quality checks.

**Operating standardisation.** This gives the group one view of field work across brands. Inbound mail follows agreed routing rules. Jobs connect to customers and sites in the same way. Cases and board measures use shared definitions.

VH3 AI deals with operating standardisation. The group can set one operating standard while each brand continues to use its existing FMS.

## What the board needs to see

Operating partners and portfolio boards need measures they can compare across the group. Typical measures include utilisation, overtime, SLA exposure, first-time-fix, quote conversion, and the time needed to bring a new bolt-on into the group operating model.

For a UK field service platform, a useful weekly pack usually includes:

| Board question | Operational signal |
|---|---|
| Are we delivering the contracted work? | Jobs completed vs open, by brand and region |
| Are engineers productive without burning out? | Utilisation, overtime, unusual engineer patterns |
| Where is service risk building? | SLA risk, repeat visits, H&S escalations |
| Is commercial work converting? | Quote and PO threads, accepted vs stalled |
| Is the front door under control? | Shared-inbox backlog, auto-triaged vs human review |
| Did the last bolt-on integrate? | Same definitions live on the new brand's jobs and mail |

These answers are useful only when each brand uses the same definitions. If "emergency" means danger to life in one brand and a next-day callout in another, the group figures cannot be compared.

## What standardisation looks like in practice

Use this order for a practical roll-up programme.

### 1. Map how work arrives

Walk each brand's shared inboxes, FM portals, and FMS job creation paths with the people who run them. Mark every handoff where mail becomes a job, a quote, or a filing decision. Time the manual hours. That map is the integration plan.

### 2. Register the mailboxes that matter

Choose the shared and team inboxes that the group workflow will process. Keep individual inboxes outside that workflow. Record the scope so each brand knows which mail is included.

### 3. Publish one reviewed taxonomy at a time

Review the category and routing rules, publish them, and assign the approved version to each mailbox. Each mailbox then uses the destinations and safety rules agreed by the group.

### 4. Connect jobs, customers, and sites

Read the FMS each brand already runs. Connect jobs to the correct customers and sites, even when names differ between systems. Where the source supports it, VH3 can load up to five years on day one. Connie and reports use that combined history.

### 5. Watch the group continuously

Sentinels flag recurring faults, SLA risk, quiet customers, and jobs that do not close cleanly. Cases give each problem an owner and supporting evidence. Local teams decide what to do next.

### 6. Put the same answers in front of every layer

Ops leads ask Connie questions in plain English. Account managers receive briefings and reports. The board pack uses the same definitions as the operation.

## How this maps to PE value creation

The work should improve measures used during the hold period:

- **Faster bolt-on integration.** Shared mailbox and taxonomy standards travel to the next acquisition instead of reinventing routing each time.
- **Lower coordination cost.** Less retyping between inbox, FMS, and spreadsheet; fewer hours assembling the weekly pack.
- **Fewer service surprises.** Sentinels and cases surface risk before a complaint or penalty.
- **Cleaner exit diligence.** Acquirers inherit an operating picture and evidence trail that already exists in the company's account.

Treat software delivery and operating standardisation as separate programmes. Faster delivery still needs good review and exception handling. The same applies when email triage becomes faster and people must own the cases that need judgment.

## Ideal fit

This guide is written for:

- Lower-mid to mid-market PE sponsors building UK field service platforms
- Operating partners who place the same playbook across multiple portcos
- Platform company COOs and ops leads absorbing regional bolt-ons
- Functional partners responsible for post-close integration

If you are a single-brand contractor consolidating the stack around one FMS, start with [Building an AI-native operating system](/guides/ai-native-operating-system). The build order is the same discipline at company scale. Buy-and-build adds the requirement that definitions and mailbox standards travel across legal entities.

## Where to start

1. Pick one brand and one shared inbox. Map how work arrives for two weeks of real volume.
2. Agree platform destinations and safety rules before you bind anything live.
3. Publish a reviewed taxonomy version, bind the mailbox, and run batch classification against held-out samples your ops lead can check.
4. Add the second brand only when the first brand's weekly pack uses the same definitions the board will see for the group.
5. Once the first brand works, add sentinels and Connie to the same shared definitions.

A standard built for the first company can be reused for the next acquisition. The intelligence remains in each company's account through the hold period and exit.
